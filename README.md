# Shrug Web

Shrug's website as a desktop you can click around: a macOS-style skin over the "Shrug desktop" design, built with the [Stageshow](https://github.com/benjitaylor/skills) kit's window chrome, message thread, rows and controls. On a phone it is an iPhone instead: the same apps, each redrawn as its iOS counterpart, with the kit's lock screen, notifications and message thread.

## Run it

```sh
npm install
npm run dev      # http://127.0.0.1:5173
npm run build    # type-checks, then builds to dist/
npm run preview  # serves the build
```

Node 20.19 or newer.

The build writes more than the app: the site in plain words inside the page itself, the link preview's tags, `robots.txt`, `sitemap.xml`, `llms.txt` and `404.html`, all from the data in `src/data/` (`tools/page.ts`). They carry the address the build is served from: on Vercel the project's own (its domain once it has one, its `vercel.app` address until then), so that a link to it unfolds with a picture that is there to fetch; anywhere else `SITE.url` in `src/data/site.ts`, or whatever `SITE_URL=https://… npm run build` says.

It is deployed on Vercel as the project `shrug-web`, at https://shrug-web.vercel.app: `npx vercel deploy --prod` from this folder puts up what is in it (`vercel.json` has the settings: files under `/assets/` cached for good, and a `noindex` header on any `*.vercel.app` address, so that only the site's own domain is indexed once it has one). Connecting the GitHub repository in Vercel's project settings would deploy every push instead.

Any other static host will do too. It should compress what it serves, may cache `/assets/` for good (those names change with their contents) and should not cache `index.html`. For an address it does not have it can serve either `404.html` or `index.html`: both send `/portfolio` and the like to that app. The page starts with a few lines of script and style written into it (the loading screen, and asking early for what it needs), so a Content-Security-Policy, if the host sets one, has to allow them.

## What's on the desktop

| Window | In the Dock as | What it holds |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Group Chat | Group Chat | Shrug's own Messages. Shrug asks the group (“Testimonials”) what it's like to work with them, and 89 people reply. |
| Portfolio | Portfolio (the Finder's face) | Every company under its logo, in Shrug's own order or by vertical, as icons or a list, with search, Back/Forward and a preview of the one selected. A search of three letters or fewer finds only what begins with it (a name, a vertical, a word of a company's line), so that “AI” is the AI vertical and not the “Mountain” in a water company's; a longer one finds it anywhere. Like every search on the site it reads past capitals, accents and which kind of apostrophe was typed (`src/apps/fold.ts`). Where someone in the group chat is behind a company, its preview has the way to what they said (see Founders and their companies, below). A company picked from somewhere else (a link, a person's card, the terminal) is brought into sight, and the window drops a search or a vertical that would hide it. |
| FAQs | FAQs (a notes app's icon) | Each question as a note, with Shrug's answer. |
| Case Studies | Case Studies | A terminal that lists the case studies, the two public ones with the rest, and links those two: to a `y`, to `please` (the Dock says “Ask nicely”), or to one read by name. The rest it says are private, with the one-pager to start on. It is a shell as far as a curious person pushes it: `help` lists what it takes, `date` is the time in San Francisco, and `ls -a` shows a file, `.rules`, that holds the rules of engagement as the FAQs give them. `whois` says who someone in the group chat is, or what a company in the portfolio does, from the site's own lists, and ends in lines that go there; `fortune` is something one of them said, as they said it. Tab finishes the word being typed, as a shell's does (twice lists what it could be), and only an empty line lets Tab move on. A private case study asked to `open` opens its company in the Portfolio, and says why. |
| About Shrug | (the menu bar's mark) | Who Shrug is, in a sentence and a few facts (when it started, its partners, which fund this is, what it backs), and counts taken from the data, each opening what it counts (the drops are VIP's: that row is a link to the points site). |

Windows drag by their bar, resize from any edge or corner, and close, minimize and zoom from their traffic lights. Drag one against the left, right or top edge to tile it, or rest the pointer on the green light for the same choices. A window keeps the place the visitor gave it: when the browser shrinks it is fitted in, and when the room comes back so does the window. A reload brings the desk back as it was left: which windows are open and where, which is in front, which are in the Dock, and what each is showing (kept for the tab, `src/os/deskmemory.ts`; a visit beginning starts as any visit does).

A new window opens where it covers least of the others, and clear of what sits on the desk: beside or under the widget, and off the icons where the desk has the room (`placed` in `src/os/wm.ts`). On a small screen (760 to 1000 points across) the conversation, which opens by itself, opens narrower than it would rather be, so that the icons can be seen and pressed. A window behind another shows what is picked in it in grey, as a Mac does; the blue is the window in front's. Bring All to Front raises what is on the desk and leaves a minimized window in the Dock.

- **With a keyboard**: everything is reachable with Tab, the arrow keys, Return and Escape. F6 steps between the menu bar, the window in front and the Dock; the window it comes to wears a ring round its frame, and along the Dock the left and right arrows go from icon to icon, Home and End to its ends. With a window focused, Alt and the arrows move it and Alt, Shift and the arrows size it. Focus goes into whatever opens and back to what opened it when it closes.
- **With a finger** (an iPad, a touch laptop): one tap opens anything; the lights, the menus and the window's edges are finger-sized wherever a touch screen is present; holding the green light shows the tiling choices.
- **The desktop's icons** open on a second click (or Return), as well as a double-click.
- **The menus** work as a Mac's do: click a title and then an item, or press on the title, draw the pointer down to an item and let go on it; let go clear of the bar and the menu goes. In an open menu a letter goes to the next item that begins with it.
- **The Dock** spaces its icons as a Mac's does, and after its rule keeps the one-pager, drawn as a sheet of paper as tall as the app icons. A count on an icon is a red circle, as iOS and macOS draw one (it widens only for two digits or more), and the dot under an open app is pale. An icon's name comes up under a pointer resting on it, not under a window being dragged across it.
- **VIP is in the Dock, and is not a window.** It sits last among the apps, before the rule, under its black tile (VIP in white capitals) and its name, and it is a link: the way to the points site (`SITE.points` in `src/data/site.ts`: a member's card, and the store their points are spent in), which opens in a new tab and says so to a screen reader. No dot lights under it and nothing opens on the desk. Everything else that names VIP leads to the same place the same way: VIP in the Go menu, `open vip` in the terminal (a line to press, as `open one-pager` gives), the Drops row in About Shrug, and “See who got one in VIP” in the Drops conversation. It was a photo library of the drops once; there is no such window now, and nothing opens one: an address that asks for it (`?open=vip`, `/vip`, or `photos`, its other name) names nothing, so the visit begins as any visit does, and a desk kept with that window on it comes back without it. The drops' pictures are still in `public/vip` and `src/data/drops.ts`, for Shrug.VC (`vc/`), which shows them.
- **The app icons** are drawn after the Mac's own as they are now, to the measure of the designs Shrug supplied: flat tiles with nothing lit along their edges (the black one keeps a faint light along its top, so that it has a shape on a dark desk), Messages a pale bubble with a nub of a tail on green, Notes a white pad with fifteen holes under its yellow binding, the Finder's face with its far side a pale pane rounded at every corner, a terminal's prompt small in the corner of a black tile, and a folder in a Mac's light blue with a sheet showing over its front (`src/os/icons.tsx`: drawn here, no file from an operating system).
- **A widget on the desk**, top left where a Mac keeps them: San Francisco. The time there, how far that is from the visitor's own clock (“Today, −8HRS”), and the weather. The time needs nothing; the weather is asked of the United States' National Weather Service (`api.weather.gov`: public, free, no key) by the visitor's own browser, when the desk is drawn and every twenty minutes while it is looked at. With no answer the widget shows the time alone: it is never shown a weather it was not told. It says what the sky is doing and not how likely the service thinks that is (“Slight Chance Rain Showers” is Rain Showers), on a second line where one is not enough. Temperatures are in Fahrenheit for a visitor whose English is from where that is used, Celsius otherwise. A desk under 1240 points wide (or 641 tall) keeps the room for its windows: the widget is there only where the conversation, opening in the middle, leaves it clear (`src/os/SanFrancisco.tsx`).
- **Five wallpapers, one to a visit, and a Wallpapers folder on the desk.** The first visit is on the picture the site has always had (Indigo), and each visit after it is on the next: Dusk, Ember, Lagoon, Graphite, and round again. A reload, or a return through the browser's history, is the same visit and keeps its wallpaper. Which one a visit shows is settled by a few lines at the head of the page, before anything is drawn, so the right picture is asked for at once and no visit shows one wallpaper and then another; the last one shown is kept in the visitor's browser for the next visit to move on from. Only on the desktop: a phone has its own wallpaper and fetches none of these, and a visit there does not move the desk's on. The folder, between Portfolio and the one-pager, opens a window laid out as a photo library of the five (its head, its tiles and its dark room for one picture go by the names they had in VIP's window, `vip…` in `styles/apps.css`; LP Updates is laid out with the same head). The one on the desk is ringed and says so. A tile opens its wallpaper large: Set as Wallpaper puts it on the desk to stay, visit after visit (it fades in over the one before it), Download hands over the file at its full size (3840 × 2160, a JPEG of about 1.4 MB, under its own name), the arrows step through the rest and Escape goes back to the grid. The switch in the window's bar, Change Each Visit, sets the desk going again, and the View menu has it too, with Next Wallpaper. `?open=wallpapers` opens the window; with `?still` a visit keeps the wallpaper the last one had.
- **Log In, and the LP Updates folder it unlocks.** At the right of the menu bar, before the clock, is Log In. It lets down a small sheet: an email address, then the six digits sent to it. Logged in, the bar shows the member's first name, and the sheet has their points, the way to the store and Log Out. Logging in is what puts the **LP Updates** folder on the desk, above the one-pager: a visitor never sees it. It holds the documents that are that member's to read, each a PDF to download, and one kept to the LPs of a single fund says so. Who sees what is not decided here: it is the points site's own system (`store/`, and Products → Visible to in Airtable), asked at `/api` (`src/os/account.ts`). Until that server is live the site is built as a demo, in which a stand-in in the browser answers: no email is sent, any six digits but 000000 are right, and the one sample member is an LP in Shrug 1. Building with `VITE_ACCOUNT=live` talks to the server instead, and needs `/api/*` on this site's address sent on to the points site (a rewrite in `vercel.json`). Only on the desktop: a phone has neither. `?open=updates` opens the window, which says it is locked to someone not logged in.
- **A notification** drops in at the top of the screen, under the menu bar and beside the desk's icons, when a reply lands while the conversation is not the window in front: who it is from, the group's name and how the reply begins. One at a time, a few seconds each and no more often than every half minute, and it waits while the pointer is on it; pressed, it opens the conversation at that reply. None lands under the pointer, where it would take a press meant for what was there: the first reply after the pointer has moved away brings one (`src/os/Notifications.tsx`).
- **The shrug emoticon** is the menu bar's mark, top left, and is nowhere else on the site: not in the tab's title, About Shrug, the screen saver or the conversation. Everywhere else Shrug is its logo (`src/data/logo.ts`). Its menu opens About Shrug and, under it, What’s New in Shrug V: the FAQs, on the question that says what is different about this fund. When the desk has nothing to give (a search of the portfolio that finds no company, a command the terminal does not know) the mark shrugs: its arms lift once and come back down (`src/os/shrug.ts`; not with reduced motion).
- **The screen saver** starts after two and a half minutes of stillness, or from the mark's menu, where it can also be set to Never (remembered). It waits while the conversation is still arriving in a window that is showing. It comes on with the first of the testimonials there to be read, and turns to the next from there. Its date is the menu bar's written out, in the same order (Friday 20 November). Any key, a click, a tap or a movement wakes it, and that key or tap does nothing else.
- **The page's title** names the window in front.

### The group chat

It is laid out as an iMessage group conversation, seen from Shrug's side:

- Shrug's messages are the blue bubbles on the right. The script types them into the message field and sends them, so each bubble leaves the field the way a sent message does, and "Delivered" comes up under the last one a moment after it has landed.
- Everyone else is gray on the left, with their name and role over the bubble and their initials beside it.
- The sidebar is the list of conversations: the group (“Testimonials”) and, under it, a few easter eggs. The 89 replies are in the group and nowhere else. Each row's name and last message are centred on its picture. Away from the group its row is marked when replies come in, and the Dock's badge counts them until it is opened again.
- Search finds the conversations, and everyone in the group: by name or role whether or not they have replied yet, and by what they said once they have said it. It reads past capitals, accents and which kind of apostrophe or quotation mark was typed. A person found goes to their reply. Someone still to reply is listed as “Hasn’t replied yet”; picking them brings in every reply and goes to theirs (passing over them with the arrow keys leaves the conversation to play).
- Shrug's question is typed and sent within about three seconds of arriving, and the first reply is in a second after that. Then every reply keeps the same time: its typing dots, the message, a breath, the next, a little over one a second. It never speeds up. The pace is `WRITING` and `BETWEEN` in `src/apps/chat.ts`.
- Nobody has to wait for them all: while replies are still arriving, **Load all 89 replies** sits at the head of the thread, on glass, with room of its own above the day the conversation begins with. It brings in every reply at once and leaves the reader where they were, with a count of what is new below them and the way down to the newest.
- While the replies are paused the thread says so in the same place: **Replies paused · Resume**. Whoever was writing holds still, their dots with them, and Resume there (or Pause Replies again, wherever it was chosen) sets them going.
- A thread that is put away while replies arrive (its window closed or minimised) is not drawn into while it cannot be seen, and is laid out afresh when it is shown again. (The kit measures a bubble as it lands, and one that lands out of sight would measure as nothing: the thread once came back with a screen of empty space under it.) A window that was closed opens again on the newest reply, wherever it had been scrolled to; one that was only minimised comes back where the reader was, with the count of what is new.
- The name in the bar, or the Details button, opens the details: pause the replies, show them all, play it again, and everyone in the group. Show Every Reply and Play From the Start close the details, so that what they did to the thread is seen. A person there opens their card, with Show in Testimonials.
- The easter eggs are short conversations of Shrug's own (Akash Nigam late at night, an unknown number, a drop on its way), in `src/data/eggs.ts`: edit them, add to them or take them out there. One the visitor writes in becomes the one last used: its row moves to the head of the others and says what they wrote, and when.
- The plus beside the field is the browser's own file picker, on the desktop and on the iPhone alike, as the plus in Messages is the way to a photo or a file: what is picked waits in the field (a picture small, a file by its name and size, each with a way back out) and goes into the conversation as a bubble of its own ahead of the words. It is shown from where it is on the visitor's device and goes nowhere, as their words do; after a reload a picture is there by its name, since a page cannot keep a file.
- **Founders and their companies.** Where someone in the group is behind a company in the portfolio, the two lead to each other: the company's preview in the Portfolio window (and its card on the phone) has the way to what they said, and their card in the details has the way to the company. Nothing is written by hand: a person's role, as Shrug gives it (“Co-Founder of Living Carbon”), names the company, whole and by itself (`src/data/founders.ts`). Thirty-one people and thirty companies find each other this way now; a role or a company added later is found the same way.
- **A link to one reply.** `?open=chat&at=tony-hawk` opens the conversation at what that person said (everyone replies at once if they had not yet). A person's card has Copy Link to Reply; on the phone it is Share Reply.
- Anything a visitor types is sent the way a message is: a blue bubble that leaves the field, with “Delivered” under the last of them. It is a picture of delivery: nothing is sent anywhere and nobody replies.
- After the last reply the conversation simply ends. There is no sign-off, and no links are left at its foot.
- A reload (or a tab a phone threw away and brought back) picks the conversation up where it was: the replies that had come in, what the visitor typed, and what had been read. Play From the Start starts it over.
- A screen reader is told when the first reply lands and then every tenth; each message is read as who, then what.
- With reduced motion set, the conversation is all there at once, opened at its top.

- **On a real phone the phone is the frame.** The screen is drawn edge to edge, under the phone's own status bar and down to its foot. Safari paints the bar above the page and the bar below it itself, each in one colour that it reads from the page (it will not put a picture there). So the page hands it both: a strip of colour fixed along the top edge of the screen and another along the foot, each in the colour of what is showing there (`src/phone/edges.ts`, drawn by `Phone.tsx`), and the wallpaper is brought to those same two colours at its own edges. The strips are made to the measure WebKit takes of one before it will use its colour (fixed, the screen across, more than 10px deep, solid, and lying over the point 4px in from the middle of the edge: the rule is in WebKit's `LocalFrameView::fixedContainerEdges`, and is written out beside the strips in `styles/phone.css`). Over the wallpaper the bars are then its own top and its own foot; over an app they are the app's ground; under a sheet they are the shade behind it. The home indicator at the foot is the site's own: the phone's is below the browser, and leaves it.
- **An app opens the way one does on an iPhone**: out of its icon (or the widget, the notification or the company's logo that was pressed), whole from the first moment and growing to fill the screen in half a second, its icon's picture growing over it without being stretched (as wide as the window, in the middle of its height) and fading as it goes, on a spring with no bounce: off from a standstill, most of the way in the first third of the time, and a long soft landing (`src/phone/Zoom.tsx`, `--spring` in `styles/ios.css`). Putting it away is the same in reverse: into its icon, or into the Messages widget if that is what the conversation was opened from. The Home Screen itself keeps still behind an app that is coming or going: its icons fade, and nothing on the screen grows or shrinks but the app (it used to fall back and return, which read as the whole screen zooming on every opening and closing). The first frame drawn after the tap is already the move. It is moved by a transform and nothing else, so the browser draws it without the page's own thread: nothing an app is doing can hold it up, or leave its window somewhere its contents are not. A pushed screen and a sliding app follow the same spring.
- **An app opened for the first time opens on a launch screen**, as an iPhone's do: a flat screen in the colour of what it is opening on (a card's own colour, the gray of a list). Its own screens are put together while it is already on its way out of its icon, and come up over that. From then on it is kept as it was left, so opening it again shows it at once. (`first` in `phone/apps/index.tsx` is what each app says it opens on. Case Studies is the exception, `eager` there: it takes typing the instant it opens, so its screen is put together with it and nothing typed is lost.)
- **Glass keeps its frost.** The Dock, the Search pill, the folders, an open folder's pane, and the Lock Screen's notification are panes of frosted glass. A browser frosts what is behind a pane only while nothing the pane is inside is itself see-through or out of focus, so nothing here is faded or blurred as a whole: the Home Screen, the Lock Screen and an open folder are moved as one, and faded and put out of focus piece by piece (each pane, each widget, each icon and each name for itself: `styles/phone.css`, `styles/phone/folder.css`). Faded as one, the glass went clear and pale for the length of every fade and came back frosted with a snap.
- **A swipe up the home indicator carries the app** up the screen under the finger, the Home Screen coming into sight behind it, and let go it goes from wherever it is into its icon in one move, its icon's picture coming up over it where it is. Taken back down, it settles back.
- **Back leads back the way in.** A company picked in the Portfolio folder opens its card in Contacts, and Back on that card leads back to the folder, open on the page it was left on, the card going back into the company's own picture there; it does not lead on into Contacts' list. A question picked in the FAQs folder does the same, its note going back into its row and fading away as it gets there. The Home Screen behind the folder is out of focus from the first frame it shows. Come to from inside the app, the same screens go back inside the app (`pop` in `phone/system.ts`).
- **One press, one move, and no waiting.** For an eighth of a second after a tap nothing takes another; after that only a second tap where the first was is turned away, so two taps do what one does while a press anywhere else is taken at once: Back while a screen is still sliding in, another app while the last is on its way into its icon. The second tap is turned away whole, from the moment it comes down (a press gives a field the keyboard before its click is sent: a double tap on an app's icon used to land in that app's search field), and for as long as the move takes: 0.4 seconds, or 0.6 where an app is coming to the front or going. The home indicator is never turned away (`watchPresses` in `phone/system.ts`).
- **Anywhere else it is drawn inside an iPhone**: in a narrow window on a computer, or with `?phone` in the address at any size. The frame, the status bar, the island and the home indicator are the kit's `Phone`, at the real proportions. There, a field that takes the keyboard brings up the kit's iOS keyboard, and its keys pop as the computer's are typed. Where a window cannot hold the frame at about two thirds of its size or more (a short window, or a laptop zoomed to 200%), the phone is drawn edge to edge instead, so nothing is cut off and zooming makes the type larger; a window too small even for that gets a Home Screen that scrolls.
- **The badge on Messages counts what is new.** It starts at nothing, ticks up with each reply that comes in to the group, and is cleared by reading the conversation, as a badge is on an iPhone. The number beside Back inside an easter egg is the same count (`useNewReplies` in `src/apps/state.ts`; the desktop's Dock counts the same way).
- **Out of the conversation, the replies come a little quicker**: about one every 1.4 seconds in place of 2, and the first of them sooner. (In sight a reply lands every two seconds: half-way between the 2.8 it began at and the 1.2 it was cut to, at which nobody could take in what was said.) On the Lock Screen and the Home Screen a reply is a notification or a line in the widget, with no message field to watch being typed into and no dots to watch being written, so those waits are cut short (`UNSEEN` in `src/apps/chat.ts`). Open the conversation and it keeps the desktop's time.
- **A folder opens where it is**, as one does on an iPhone: the Home Screen falls out of focus behind it and it grows into a larger piece of glass with its name over it. Portfolio shows the companies nine to a page, each under its logo the way a folder holds apps, and turns sideways; FAQs lists the questions, all ten in sight on a phone with its browser's bars showing (402 by 700); on a shorter screen they scroll, and the list fades out at the end there is more past. Picking one opens it in its app (a company at its card in Contacts; a question in Notes), and a tap outside, the home bar or the browser's back closes the folder (`src/phone/Folder.tsx`). The strip of dots under Portfolio's pages steps back and on from its left and right halves. The folders are large wherever they fit; on a shorter screen they are icon-sized folders among the icons, and only on a very short one are they left off (Contacts and Notes are in the Dock).
- **Every move is a step in the browser's history.** Safari's swipe from the left edge (or an Android back button) is the phone's Back: it goes back a screen inside an app, closes a sheet, and leaves an app for the Home Screen. Search and the App Switcher are steps too, so Back closes them. An app sent home steps back to the Home Screen's own step, however it was opened and however far it was gone into (the step is written again with each app as it was left), so Back on the Home Screen never opens again the app that was just left, and a visit to an app leaves no steps behind it. A reload comes back to where it was and as it was (open, or on its Lock Screen if it had not been opened yet or had locked itself), with what the visitor had made: their notes and folders, a message to Shrug (what was being written, and what had been sent), and the conversation as far as it had got (kept in the browser for the tab; nothing is sent anywhere).
- **While something is opening, closing or sliding, presses on what is under it are ignored**, as on an iPhone, so a double tap does what one tap does.
- **A change of layout keeps the visitor's place.** Turn a tablet, drag a window across the line, or open a split view, and the app that was in front is in front in the other layout, on the same conversation, company or question, with no Lock Screen in between (`src/os/crossing.ts`). A keyboard that shortens the page is not a change of layout.
- **Apps open where an iPhone would have left them**: Notes inside FAQs, one step in from its first screen, with Back leading out; Contacts on its list. A company's card that is come to from outside the app (search, the Home Screen's folder, a link) is over a list brought to that company, so Back finds it among its own. After that they stay as they were left, as they do on an iPhone, until they are flicked out of the App Switcher. An app that fails does what one does on an iPhone: it closes to the Home Screen and starts afresh next time; the rest of the phone carries on.
- **Turned on its side**, the apps reflow as apps do. A real iPhone keeps its Lock Screen and Home Screen upright; a page turns with the browser, so those two are laid out for the width instead, with the Dock down the trailing edge.
- **Search inside an app** is at the foot of its list, in Messages, Contacts and Notes. While it is on, the button beside the field is the one that ends it (✕, where compose was); ended, the list is back where it was left. With nothing found all three say the same: “No Results for “…””, the words cut short where they run on, and what to try. Notes compares what is typed with what is written as a reader would: capitals, accents and the cut of a quotation mark make no difference (the notes are typeset, “don’t”, and a keyboard types "don't"), and a question opened from what was found shows where, the words marked and brought into sight. Messages finds the conversation with Shrug as well, by its name, by what was written in it and by the name of a file sent. The field's ring is a keyboard's: a finger put in it is shown the caret and nothing more.
- **A screen's name gives way to its buttons.** The two sides of a bar are never narrower than what is in them, so a name too long for the room between (a folder the visitor named at length) is cut short, and does not run on under Back.
- **Small gray type is a shade darker than iOS's own** (in the conversation on the desktop too), so that it meets the contrast guidance. The blue is iOS's, which on white falls a little short of that guidance for small text, as it does on an iPhone.
- **Added to a phone's Home Screen** the site has Shrug's icon (`public/apple-touch-icon.png`, drawn by `tools/icons.cjs`) and still opens in the browser. Running it full screen from there is left off (`"display": "browser"` in `public/manifest.webmanifest`): in that mode the phone's status bar keeps one colour of ink, which can't be right over both the wallpaper and a white app. It also hasn't been tried on a real phone.
- **Search** (the pill above the Dock, or a pull down on the Home Screen) looks through the people who have replied so far, the companies and the questions (all by the rule the Portfolio's own search goes by: three letters or fewer find what a word begins with, so “ai” is the AI companies and Mem.ai, and not a Chainsmoker), the apps under either of their names (Contacts is also "portfolio", Notes is "faqs"), and the two links that sit among the apps, the one-pager and VIP; it opens the app at what was found, or the link in a new tab. "contact", "email" and the like find the way to Shrug. Shrug's own name, "about", "team", "who", "partners" or a partner's name put Shrug's card in Contacts first, and "what's new", "new" or "shrug v" the note on what is different about Shrug V. With nothing found there are still two things to press: the one-pager, and Ask in Messages. While it is up the Home Screen falls away almost to nothing behind it, so its white type reads over anything. The field runs the width of the screen over the keyboard, as Spotlight's does, and what Return will do is written in it after what has been typed (“— Open”). A title runs to a second line before it is cut short, so a question is read whole. A tap on the ground behind it closes search, and so does a pull down on what is found, as on an iPhone; a button that says Close Search is there for a keyboard and a screen reader. Only the list is dragged, and it holds a drag at its ends (it gives and springs back); dragging it puts the keyboard away. On a phone the keyboard is search's own, drawn on the page (the phone's would push the page about as it came and went): the first drag on what is found lowers it and the field drops to a pill at the foot of the screen, as Spotlight's does, a tap on the field brings it back, and with it down a pull closes search. The ground behind search is one dark colour from the very top of the screen to its foot, and the browser's bars are given the same.
- **The keyboard.** With it up there is more page than screen, and a browser will slide the page about under a finger; here only what scrolls is dragged. A browser says how much the keyboard took only once it has finished moving, a third of a second after it set off, so the screen is laid out when the keyboard sets off instead: a field taking it is put where the last keyboard left it, and one letting go gives the screen its whole height back at once, under the keyboard as it goes (`src/phone/Phone.tsx`).
- **Companies' logos are fetched ahead** of Contacts being opened, a few at a time once the phone is open and idle, so its list opens with its faces in it (not for a visitor who has asked their browser to save data).
- **The link preview** is the conversation's opening (`public/og.png`, taken by `tools/og.cjs`), so the address unfolds as the chat when it is texted.
- **The page's title** names the screen (the conversation, the company, the app), so each step in the browser's history, and a screen reader arriving, says where it is.

To send someone the site as a single file they can open on a phone or in a browser: `node tools/preview.cjs shrug.html`. Opened on a computer, the file shows the iPhone, with a button for the desktop.

### Arriving, and when it goes wrong

Whatever happens on the way in, there is something on screen and a way on.

- **While it loads**: the mark and a bar on the dark ground, in place of an empty page (nothing is drawn at all if the site arrives within half a second). After ten seconds it says it is still loading and offers the one-pager; after thirty it offers another go. It is plain HTML and CSS in `index.html`, so it is there before any script.
- **If a script or stylesheet does not arrive**, or arrives and cannot run: "Shrug didn’t load", with Try Again, the one-pager and the plain version.
- **In a browser too old to draw the site** (it needs `:has()`, `color-mix()`, container units and `inert`: Safari 16.4, Chrome 111, Firefox 121, or newer): "Shrug needs a newer browser", with the one-pager and the plain version, and Continue Anyway if the site did manage to start underneath.
- **The plain version** is the site in plain words: who Shrug is, twelve of the testimonials, every company with its link and line, the answered FAQs, the one-pager. It is written into `#root` at build time from the data, so it is what a browser with scripts off shows, what a search engine or a link preview reads, and what the two cards above uncover. The site replaces it when it starts. `llms.txt` is the same in full, as text.
- **The desktop and the iPhone are separate downloads**, and the page asks for the one its screen needs alongside the first script. The other is fetched once things are quiet, so a turned tablet or a narrowed window has it to hand. If it still cannot be had (the connection has gone, or a new version of the site has replaced the files), a card says so over the wallpaper, tries again by itself when the connection returns, and never trades itself for the browser's own error page; going back to the size that was working brings that layout back.
- **Phones fetch only what a phone shows** (not the desktop's wallpaper), and where Apple's own type is not installed the site's is asked for at once and never swapped in under a reader.

### Links

- `?open=portfolio,faqs` opens those windows as well as the group chat (on a phone, it arrives in the last one named, already unlocked; `chat` arrives in the conversation itself). Names: `chat`, `portfolio` (or `contacts`; `wallet` and `files`, which it has been, still open it), `faqs` (or `notes`), `case-studies`, `about` (or `settings`). VIP is not among them: it is a link to the points site and has no window or app to open, so `vip` (and `photos`, which it also answered to) names nothing. Alone, it leaves the visit to begin as any does; beside other names, the others open. About Shrug is the desktop's: a phone asked for it opens Shrug's own card in Contacts, where the same is said (asked for Settings, which an old link may name, it opens on its Home Screen).
- **The address follows the visitor.** On both layouts it names where they are: the app, and the company, question or conversation open in it (`?open=portfolio&at=eight-sleep`, `?open=faqs&at=4`, `?open=chat&at=drops`). A person's name there is a way in and not somewhere the address stays: `?open=chat&at=tony-hawk` opens the conversation at their reply. The iPhone writes it with each step it takes through history, the desktop as its front window changes, so the link in the address bar, or one passed on with Share, opens there. Whatever else the address carries (`?phone`, `?draft`) is left as it was (`src/os/place.ts`). The desk as a visit finds it, the Lock Screen and the Home Screen are the site's plain address.
- Addresses are read forgivingly (`src/os/address.ts`): the switches answer in any case (`?OPEN=Portfolio`), names can be set apart with commas, semicolons, plus signs or spaces, a path or a hash names an app too (`/portfolio`, `#faqs`), and a switch can be turned off (`?phone=0`). On a host that serves `404.html` for an address it does not have, that page sends `/portfolio` and the like to the app and anything else to the site.
- `?phone` shows the iPhone whatever the size of the screen, drawn in its frame. `?phone=hand` draws it edge to edge instead, as a real phone gets it, for trying that layout in a browser on a computer.
- `?saver` starts on the screen saver (desktop only). `?idle=20` sets how many seconds of stillness start it, and lock the phone.
- `?draft` is the editor's view: what Shrug has still to supply (an FAQ's answer, should a question be added without one) is drawn as the note that says so. Without it a visitor is shown a line written for them (see Content).

**Shrug's own pictures.** A folder of the companies' logos and a folder of the photographs of the people in the testimonials are taken in by one tool: `python3 tools/pictures.py --logos DIR --people DIR`, then `python3 tools/logo-colours.py` for the cards' colours. Each file is matched to its company or person by the name in its own (a number before it is passed over, and so is a company after a person's name; it says which it could not match, and guesses at none), cut to size (a logo at 176 pixels, twice the most it is drawn across, and again at 430 for the head of its card; a face at 256 square, cut from the middle), given a plain lower-case name and written to `public/logos` and `public/people` as WebP; a file with REVIEW in its name is left out and listed, and where two files are for one company neither is used until one is picked (`--pick`): they are listed and drawn side by side (`--sheet`); what was matched is written to `src/data/pictures.ts`, which the data reads. **Reaching Shrug.** `reach()` in `site.ts` is the one way to Shrug everything uses: an email if `SITE.contact.email` is set, a text if `contact.sms` is, and with neither the one-pager. The day an address is written there, these all use it with no other change: the terminal (`y` opens a message asking for access), an FAQ with no answer yet, About Shrug (a link to write appears beside the one-pager), and search ("contact", "email").

**A message to Shrug.** The compose button in Messages on the iPhone starts a message whose one possible recipient is Shrug, with files picked in the browser's own picker (a phone offers its photos, its camera and its files): up to ten, 25 MB each. It is meant for a deck or a one-pager. Where it goes is `SITE.contact.inbox` in `site.ts`: an address that takes a form by POST, the words as `text` and each file as `files`, and answers 2xx once it has them. Shrug's plan is for these to go to a forwarding number linked to an email account, which is to be built later; none is set yet, so a message written there is marked Not Delivered a moment after it is sent, says why when its mark is pressed (and offers the one-pager there), and nothing leaves the page; it never says Delivered of something that was not. With an address set it is sent there, shows how far it has got, and says Delivered when the address has answered (`src/phone/apps/outbox.ts`).

What is being written there, and what has been sent, is kept for the tab as the visitor's notes are (`sessionStorage`): the words, and the names and sizes of the files; not the files themselves, nor one that was only picked. So a reload brings the conversation back: a message that was still on its way comes back as not delivered, a picture comes back as a file with its name, and a message whose files went with the page says so if it is tried again, and is not sent without them. The conversation's stamp gives the day and the time its first message was sent, as every conversation's does, and the mark that says a message did not go sits beside the last part of it. A file that is turned away (over 25 MB, or an eleventh) is said for about four seconds and then taken back.

**To check from a machine that can reach them:** 60 of the companies' links are `http://`, as shrug.vc's portfolio page has them. They were left so because they could not be tried from where this was built, and a wrong `https://` is a dead link where a browser would have upgraded the `http://` one itself. Two testimonials have slips to check against their authors' own text (Ashton Kutcher: "a entrepreneurs mind"; Maggie Norris: "a trusted sounding board and mentors"), and `caseStudies.ts` lists Feastables, which is not among the 81 companies.

One company has no logo, Sprinter: its site has no icon the two icon services could find, and Shrug has not supplied one. Lula's is the icon of lula.com, which a funding profile gives as its address; the Shrug page links only to its case study. About a quarter of the other logos come from small icons (32 to 48px) and are soft at the preview's size; a proper logo file replaces any of them the same way.

Nothing leaves the page, bar one thing asked for: the desk's widget asks the United States' weather service for San Francisco's weather, which tells that service a browser asked and nothing about who. A message typed into the group chat is shown on screen, marked Delivered, and goes nowhere, on the desktop and on the iPhone alike, and the terminal no longer asks for an email it has nowhere to send. What a visitor makes (their notes, drafts, how far the conversation had got) is kept in their own browser so that a reload does not lose it, and is sent nowhere. The site does not say so anywhere itself now (the iPhone's Settings did, under Privacy, and has gone with the app): if it stops being true, analytics added by a host included, it will need saying somewhere.

- **The desktop and the iPhone are separate downloads.** `os/Desktop.tsx` is the root and loads `os/Surface.tsx` (the desktop) or `phone/Phone.tsx` (the iPhone), whichever the screen shows; what they share (the conversation, the data, the kit) is loaded once. `os/Boundary.tsx` keeps a failure inside the window or app it happened in.
- **Window behaviour** is in `os/wm.ts` (state) and `os/WindowFrame.tsx` (dragging, resizing, the lights).

- **A new app on the iPhone**: write it in `phone/apps/` from the parts in `phone/ui.tsx`, name it in `phone/apps/index.tsx`, draw its icon in `phone/icons.tsx`, and put it on the Home Screen or in the Dock (`DOCKED`, `ON_HOME`).
- **The wallpaper** is a picture, `public/wallpaper.webp` for the desktop and `public/wallpaper-phone.webp` for the iPhone, made from a drawing: `tools/wallpaper.svg` and `tools/wallpaper-phone.svg` (Shrug's own, a sky that runs from near-black at the top to a pale blue at the foot). To change it, change or replace the drawings and run `node tools/wallpaper.cjs`: it draws each at the size it is shown and gives it a fine grain, so that the long gradients do not band once the picture is compressed (it needs Playwright, and Python with numpy and Pillow). `tools/wallpaper.py` adds that grain, and can still draw the four wallpapers the site had before (`glass`, `sheets`, `dusk` and `present`: `python3 tools/wallpaper.py public/wallpaper.webp 2880 1800 86 glass`). The colours that follow the wallpaper are the fallbacks under it in `os.css` and `phone.css`, the two colours the phone's wallpaper is faded to at its top and foot in `phone.css`, and `WALLPAPER_TOP`, `WALLPAPER_FOOT` and `WALLPAPER_DIMMED` in `src/phone/chrome.ts` (what a phone's browser is given to paint its bars with over the Lock Screen and Home Screen): a new wallpaper needs all of them brought to its own top and foot. The loading screen keeps the site's own dark, and `theme-color` in `index.html` is that ground's. To use a photograph instead, put it in `public/` and set `--wallpaper: url('/wallpaper.jpg')` on `.os`. The desktop's other wallpapers are the same drawing in other colours: `COLOURWAYS` in `tools/wallpaper.cjs` turns every colour of the drawing but its white round the colour wheel, and the tool makes each three times over (as the desk shows it, small for the Wallpapers window, and at 3840 × 2160 to download, in `public/wallpapers`) and writes the list the site reads, `src/data/wallpapers.ts`, and the same pictures into the script at the head of `index.html` that settles which one a visit shows. To add one, add a line there and run the tool; to use a picture of Shrug's own in a colourway's place, put its three sizes in `public/wallpapers` under that colourway's names (or change the names in `wallpapers.ts`).
- **The portfolio's pictures.** A company's logo is a file in `public/logos`, named in `src/data/portfolio.ts`. On the iPhone a company's contact card is a page in its logo's colour with the logo in a round face: `python3 tools/logo-colours.py` reads both from the logos (the ground each is drawn on, and the logo's own colour taken down to where white words read on it) and writes `src/data/logoColours.ts`; run it again when a logo is added or changed. A company with a photograph to show has it head its card in place of the logo: put the picture in `public/posters/` (taller than it is wide, 1200 pixels across or more, its subject in the upper two thirds, since the name and the buttons are set over the lower third), name it against the company's key in `POSTERS` in `src/data/portfolio.ts`, and run the same tool, which reads the colours the card takes from the picture. None has been supplied yet, so every card is headed by its logo.
- **The Lock Screen's clock** is drawn by `phone/LockClock.tsx` from outlines of the figures in `phone/clockFace.ts`, which `python3 tools/clock-face.py` writes from the Inter the site ships (it needs `fonttools` and `brotli`). Its size is in `styles/phone.css`, against the width of the screen.
- **The look** is a set of kit tokens on `.os` in `os.css`: accent, grays, type, window radius.
- **Dates and times** are written in English whatever the browser's language, with the visitor's own clock, 12 hours or 24 (`os/dates.ts`: every formatter comes from there). The date in the menu bar and on the Lock Screen is the short one, weekday first: Wed 18 Nov.
- **The focus ring** is one rule in `base.css`: the accent, solid. A surface it would not show against (the wallpaper, the Dock, a dark window) sets `--ring: #fff`. The ring is for a keyboard: what the site itself gives the keyboard to after a touch or a press of the mouse, or before anything has been pressed at all (a reload), wears none, and the next key pressed brings it back (`os/focus.ts`).

## Shrug.VC (`vc/`)

The firm's own site is kept here too, in a folder of its own: an ordinary website, not a desktop. It is
built and deployed by itself, and shares nothing with the desktop site but what it says.

```
npm run dev:vc      # work on it, at http://127.0.0.1:5173
npm run build:vc    # type-check, build into vc/dist, and write each page as HTML
```

- **Pages.** Quotes (`/`), Portfolio (`/portfolio`), VIP (`/vip`) and FAQ (`/faq`), named in
  `vc/src/router.tsx`. Each is a real address with HTML of its own (`vc/prerender.mjs` draws every page
  ahead of time, with its title, description and link-preview picture), so a link, a reload or a search
  engine is given the page itself, and all of it is there with no script. Once the site is running, a link
  between its pages swaps the page in place. Quotes, Portfolio and VIP have no title to look at (the bar
  names the page) and a heading for assistive tech only; FAQ's opening statement is its heading.
- **What it says** is read from the same data as the desktop site (`src/data`: the quotes, the portfolio,
  the FAQs, the drops' photographs), so the two never disagree, and none of it is reworded here. The
  pictures it shows (`public/people`, `public/vip`) are copied into the build (the drops' photographs as
  `/drops`, since `/vip` is a page here). What is this site's own is in `vc/src/content.ts`.
- **The look** is the approved redesign ("direction F"): strict black and white, in a light and a dark
  theme. Every colour is a token at the top of `vc/src/styles.css`, and the class `th-dark` on `<html>`
  swaps them. Text is DM Sans; Unbounded is used for the four page names in the menu's panel and nowhere else;
  DM Mono sets the small type (the page names in the bar, the filters, the labels on FAQ, the foot),
  marks placeholders and sets the initials of someone with no photograph. The wordmark and
  its arrow are the kit's own outlines (`vc/src/brand.ts`) and are not to be redrawn: the wordmark stands
  at the left of the bar and at the foot of the menu's panel, and the favicon and the link-preview picture
  (`vc/public/favicon.svg`, `social.png`) are drawn from it.
- **The theme.** A first visit follows the visitor's system setting. A choice made in the menu's panel is kept
  (`shrug-theme` in `localStorage`) and holds on every page from then on. The short script in the head of
  `vc/index.html` sets the class before anything is painted, so that nobody in the dark sees a white page
  first; `vc/src/theme.ts` takes over from it, and the two must be kept in step.
- **The way round** (`vc/src/Menu.tsx`) is one bar, at every width (the "header bar" layout, which
  replaced a rail down the left). It stays at the top of the window, 70px tall, in the page's own colour
  with a hairline under it: the wordmark on the left (to Quotes); on the right the four pages by name and
  two lines. Under 900px the names are not there and the two lines are the way round. The two lines open
  a panel from the right at any width; the panel has a cross of its own, which stands where the two lines
  are. It is a dialog (the keyboard goes into it and stays in it, Escape shuts it, the page behind does
  not scroll) and holds the case studies and the cross, Light / Dark on the row under, the four pages,
  and the wordmark with X and Instagram. Merch, shrug.vip and the email address are in neither the panel
  nor the foot: Shrug had them taken out.
- **The container and its columns.** Quotes, VIP and FAQ sit in a container as wide as the window less
  the page's margins, up to 1760px, and centred in anything wider. From 900px what is in it is laid on
  twelve equal columns 24px apart: the left of any row (a name, a label) takes the first four and the
  right (a quote, an answer) the next seven. Narrower, things stack. Portfolio's grid runs from edge to
  edge of the window, straight under the bar. The foot is a hairline, the copyright line, and X and
  Instagram.
- **Coming in.** The first time a thing is seen, hairlines draw from their left end and words rise into
  place (`styles.css`, "Coming in"). What is in view as a page opens goes in turn from the top; what is
  further down waits and plays as it is scrolled to. The short script at the foot of `vc/index.html`
  says when each thing is in view, so that it works on the page as written, before the site's own script
  has arrived. With no script, or for a visitor who has asked for less motion, nothing waits and
  everything is there from the start.
- **Quotes** shows everyone with a testimonial, in the data's order, each in full, and can be narrowed to
  founders (those Shrug funded) or investors (everyone else) with the filters at its head: words in the
  small type, the chosen one in square brackets. The counts are of the data.
- **Portfolio** is the companies in `src/data/portfolio.ts`, less four Shrug asked to be left off this site
  (Layer2, The Network, Lula and YOLO: `LEFT_OFF` in `vc/src/content.ts`; the desktop site still lists
  them), as an edge-to-edge grid of squares: 75 tiles. A tile lit (a pointer over it, the keyboard on it,
  or the first tap on a touch screen; the second tap follows the link) shows the company's sector, name
  and write-up. At rest a tile shows the company's own wordmark, as Founders Fund's portfolio does, and not
  its app icon: 69 taken from the head of the company's own website and six from files Shrug sent (Cameo,
  Cloud23, Enigma, Alt, Poparazzi, HQ Trivia), all kept in `vc/public/companies/` as a picture of the
  mark's shape alone, which the page draws in the colour of its text in either theme (`vc/src/marks.ts`
  lists them and says how to replace one). A lit tile shows the company's photograph: six
  are in (Varda, Hadrian, Senra, AMCA, Avalanche Energy, Eight Sleep), from files Shrug sent. **Still
  placeholder:** every other lit tile shows a dark fill marked `[Photo: …]`; to put a photograph in, save
  it (900px or more on its shorter side) in `vc/public/companies/` and name it against the company's key
  in `PHOTOS` at the top of `vc/src/pages/Portfolio.tsx`. Fifteen companies have no
  write-up in the data; their tiles show the sector and the name.
- **VIP** is a wall of cards in columns, in the order `vc/src/wall.ts` gives them. The 33 photographs are
  real (the ones in `src/data/drops.ts`, each under its drop's name), and so is the one text card about the
  Deep Tech Mat. **Still placeholder:** no videos, posts or press pieces have been supplied, so two video
  cards, three post cards and one press card stand on the wall with their missing parts in square brackets
  (`[Name]`, `[@handle]`, `[Time · Date]` …), to show how each kind looks. To replace one, give the card
  its real fields in `EXTRAS` in `vc/src/wall.ts` (the file says which) and put its files in
  `vc/public/wall/`; a card with a link gets the arrow and becomes a link.
- **FAQ** is a stack of sections, each a label in the small type beside what it labels: an opening
  statement in large type (the page's heading), then every question and answer in `src/data/faqs.ts`,
  numbered, under a hairline and all open, and last a line sending the reader on to shrug.vip.
- **The case studies** are one PDF. Put it at `vc/public/shrug-case-studies.pdf` and build again: the
  menu's "Case studies PDF" then downloads it, and `/case-studies` already redirects to it
  (`vc/vercel.json`). Until the file is there the link goes to `https://www.shrug.vc/case-studies`, the
  page the case studies are on today, so this site should not take over that address before the PDF exists.
- **Deploying.** `vc/dist` is the whole site, and `vc/vercel.json` goes with it (addresses without
  `.html`, `/drops` sent on to `/vip`, and how long things are kept).

## Accessibility

- **Keyboard.** Both layouts work without a pointer. Menus and lists answer the arrow keys; sheets, folders, menus and the screen saver hold the keyboard while they are up and give it back to what opened them. A page that is only read (an FAQ's answer, in the window and on the phone) takes the keyboard itself, so that the arrows and Page Down scroll a long one. The iPhone has two buttons only a keyboard or a screen reader meets, Home Screen and App Switcher, which show when they have focus.
- **Screen readers.** The page's title names the place; every screen has a heading; the conversation reads as who then what, and says when replies have come in without reading out all 89 of them.
- **Time.** Nothing on the site times out for good: the screen saver can be set to Never and waits for the conversation; the phone locks itself after two minutes but never under a reader, and opens again where it was at a tap or a key; replies can be paused from the conversation's details and from the mark's menu.
- **Reduced motion.** Nothing zooms, slides or glides; the conversation arrives whole. A tap on the Lock Screen lights the words that say how it opens, for a second, in place of lifting the screen.
- **Forced colours** (Windows High Contrast): edges, states and controls are drawn in the system's colours (`styles/access.css`).
- **Zoom.** The desktop holds together to 200% on a laptop; past that the site is the iPhone, drawn edge to edge, which reflows.
- **Contrast.** Small gray type is a shade darker than the systems' own so that it passes; iOS's blue on white falls a little short for small text, as it does on an iPhone.

## What it was tested on

In November 2026 seven testers went over the whole site, each as a different visitor, and what they found was fixed: `docs/audit.md` is the record, finding by finding. The checks they wrote are in `tests/` (`sh tests/all.sh` builds the site and runs them all: see `tests/README.md` for what that needs).

Playwright's Chromium at every size from 280px phones to a 4K desktop, with touch, with a keyboard only, slowed down, throttled to 3G, offline, with scripts off, with reduced motion and with forced colours; and WebKitGTK (Safari's engine) for the things engines differ on. Not on real iPhones, Androids or iPads, not in Firefox, and not with a real screen reader: what a real device adds (its keyboard, its safe areas, its own gestures) is emulated or argued in the code's comments.

Since the audit the site has been reworked five times from Shrug's notes: the new wallpaper, logo and FAQs; the portfolio on the phone as Contacts (after Files, and then a Wallet); a quicker conversation with Load all and Delivered; notifications on the desktop; glass on the Lock Screen and the Dock; the phone drawn to the top and foot of the screen; no Settings app; VIP as one grid; and then, from seventeen screenshots of an iPhone 17 Pro, the phone's screens set to that phone's own measure (the Lock Screen's clock, the Home Screen, search, Notes, Contacts and its cards, VIP's library and viewer, and the icons of Messages, Contacts and Notes); and then what happens between apps: how one opens and is put away, what is seen while it loads, presses made while something is still moving, the keyboard coming and going, a message to Shrug with files, and on the desktop the icons redrawn and a widget; and then Shrug's own logos, photographs and VIP pictures, search with a keyboard of its own that can be put away, the App Switcher's cards fanned as an iPhone's, and a screen that keeps still while an app opens and closes; and then VIP, which had been a photo library of the drops on both layouts, became the way to the points site: a link in each Dock, with no window or app behind it. The checks were brought up to date with each change and pass in Chromium. The passes in Safari's engine were rewritten to match but could not be run where that work was done, and none of it has been looked at on a real iPhone. Four things there can only be judged on one: how Safari paints its bars from the strips of colour at the screen's edges (the whole of "full bleed"), how the glass reads over the wallpaper, how an app feels to open and to carry off (the move is the browser's own to draw now, which is what a phone needs, but it was timed on a computer), and what the screen does as the keyboard comes and goes (written to how Safari behaves, and not seen there). The weather service could not be reached from where this was built: the widget was tried against answers in its form, and falls back to the time alone if the real ones differ. A review of the whole site as a visitor uses it followed, in four parts: what it says and where it leads nowhere, the phone's apps, the desktop, and the phone's own screens frame by frame (the Lock Screen, the Home Screen, what happens between apps, the App Switcher, banners, search). What it found was put right, and a few things were added on the way (founders and their companies leading to each other, a link to one reply, `whois` and `fortune` in the terminal, menus that work as a Mac's do, a swipe along the home indicator); the checks in `tests/checks/` hold to each of those. What the review found that is Shrug's to decide (wording inside people's testimonials, figures the FAQs give, who is named as a partner) was left as it is and listed for Shrug. The sizes are measured from the screenshots, which are of the phone's own apps and so of its whole screen: in a browser the page is a little shorter than that, by the browser's own bars.

## The kit

`src/kit/` is a copy of the kit from the Stageshow skill in `.agents/skills/stageshow/`, with no changes, so it can be replaced with a newer copy. The kit's parts are drawn for scripted demos; here they are wrapped so they work as a real interface:

- The kit's `Window` draws the chrome. `WindowFrame` puts working traffic lights over the spot the kit keeps for them and adds dragging and resizing.
- The kit's `Thread`, `Bubble`, `Stamp` and `Receipt` draw the conversation, its typing dots and its bubbles leaving the field. `apps/chat-ui.tsx` lets it scroll and keeps it on the newest reply.
- The kit's `Row` and `Avatar` draw the list of conversations and the monograms.
- The kit's `Popover` draws the menus and the details. The menu bar adds the items, keyboard and focus handling.
- The kit's `Phone`, `LockScreen` and `Notification` draw the iPhone's frame, its Lock Screen and its banners. `phone/Springboard.tsx` adds the gestures: the swipe that unlocks, the home indicator, the App Switcher. An app opens out of its icon with the site's own zoom (`phone/Zoom.tsx`) in place of the kit's `Expand`, which uncovers an app from its top edge down.

The app icons, folder and document are drawn for this site; the wallpaper and the logo are Shrug's. On the desktop the group chat's icon is drawn after the Messages icon (a white speech bubble on green), Portfolio's after the Finder's face as macOS draws it now, and FAQs' after the Notes icon (`src/os/icons.tsx`). On the iPhone the icons of Messages, Contacts and Notes are drawn after the iOS icons of those apps (`src/phone/icons.tsx`). No file from an operating system is used. The company logos belong to the companies.
